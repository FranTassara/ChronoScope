2. VALIDATION DESIGN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  GSE3416 constitutes the most phylogenetically distant external
  validation dataset available for CRS-AI. Its novelty relative to
  the training corpus (and to the zebrafish LODO validation) is
  multi-dimensional:

    * Different kingdom:  CRS-AI was trained and validated
      exclusively on animal transcription-translation feedback
      loop (TTFL) circadian data (mouse liver, Drosophila clock
      neurons, human blood, zebrafish whole organism). The plant
      circadian oscillator is built from an entirely distinct
      molecular repressilator (CCA1/LHY <-> PRR7/9 <-> TOC1/GI),
      with zero sequence or structural homology to Per/Cry/Bmal1/
      Clock. Strong performance here cannot be explained by any
      clock-gene-specific or animal-specific learned pattern.

    * Different experimental design:  a single 24h diurnal cycle
      (6 timepoints, 4h intervals) with true biological triplicate
      replication per timepoint, vs. the 48h/no-replicate and
      48h/2-replicate designs used for training and the zebrafish
      validation respectively.

    * Different experimental platform:  GPL198 Affymetrix ATH1
      Genome Array (plant-specific probe design) vs. the mammalian/
      insect/teleost Affymetrix, Illumina and Agilent platforms
      used in training and prior validation.

    * Zero data leakage:  GSE3416 was never accessed at any step
      of CRS-AI training, hyperparameter optimisation, or feature
      engineering. Labels were assigned entirely from an independent
      BioCycle analysis (RhythmicDB, E-GEOD-3416) after training
      was complete.

  Label quality: BioCycle-consistent non-rhythmic-from-absence
  labeling is used -- the same strategy used for CRS-AI v6 training
  and for the GSE37332 zebrafish validation, ensuring methodological
  consistency across all cross-species evaluations reported in
  the manuscript.

5. INTERPRETATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  The AUROC exceeds 0.85, demonstrating strong cross-kingdom
  generalisation of CRS-AI to a plant circadian oscillator.

  External LODO context: CRS-AI was trained exclusively on
  animal (mouse, Drosophila, human) data and previously validated
  on zebrafish -- all TTFL-based circadian clocks. An AUROC of
  0.920 [95% CI 0.897, 0.940] on held-out
  Arabidopsis data -- a kingdom, molecular clock architecture and
  platform not seen at any training step -- provides direct
  evidence that CRS-AI's features capture organism-agnostic
  statistical signatures of periodic oscillation (waveform shape,
  spectral concentration, cosinor fit quality) rather than any
  clock-gene-specific or animal-specific pattern.

  Label quality is supported by GI/GIGANTEA (Q=0.023, T=23.2h) -- a core
  component of the plant circadian clock -- landing in R-class,
  while LHY, TOC1 and PRR5 (borderline, 0.05<Q<0.20) illustrate
  the expected attenuation of clock-gene amplitude in a single
  24h whole-rosette time course.

  Short-cycle caveat: this design covers a single 24h cycle
  (6 timepoints) rather than the 48h/multi-cycle designs used
  for training and the zebrafish validation, which can reduce
  the discriminative power of period-fit features for genes
  with noisy single-cycle waveforms.

  Bootstrap CI width reflects sample size (N=1315 instances,
  R=1015, N=300). For manuscript reporting, cite the
  CI rather than the point estimate alone.

  Suggested reporting (JBR Methods section):
    "To test generalisation beyond Kingdom Animalia, CRS-AI was
    applied to GSE3416 (Arabidopsis thaliana, Col-0 rosette leaf,
    LD cycle; GPL198 Affymetrix ATH1 array; n=1315 genes:
    R=1015, N=300). Gene labels were derived independently
    from RhythmicDB / BioCycle (E-GEOD-3416; Q<=0.05, period
    20-28 h for rhythmic class; absent-from-database criterion
    for non-rhythmic class). The model achieved
    AUROC=0.920 (95% CI 0.897-0.940) on this zero-overlap
    external dataset from a different kingdom with a
    molecularly distinct circadian oscillator."
